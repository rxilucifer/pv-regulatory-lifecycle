This is a practice document. DMX-101 is a fictional drug and all data is invented. The headings follow the risk management plan chapter of the Indian MAH guidance (R-11), and the safety specification and pharmacovigilance plan follow ICH E2E (R-05).

## 1. Product overview
DMX-101 is a once-daily tablet (10 mg and 25 mg) used as an add-on treatment for type 2 diabetes in India. Details are in PV-001.

## 2. Safety specification
### 2.1 Epidemiology, non-clinical and clinical trial exposure
No epidemiology, non-clinical or clinical trial data is included in this exercise. These parts cannot be completed.

### 2.2 Populations not studied
No pre-approval trial data exists here, so no population can be shown as studied. Pregnancy, breastfeeding and patients under 18 are listed as missing information because the exercise holds no data on them.

### 2.3 Post-marketing experience
486 usable reports were reviewed after duplicates and rows with a missing or impossible age were set aside (PV-004). Rash was the most frequently reported event that is not in the label (58 reports). It was reported more often at 25 mg and in patients aged 65 and over (PV-005).

### 2.4 Summary of safety concerns
| Category | Concern | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Important identified risk | Nausea, headache | Listed in the reference safety information (PV-001) |
| Important potential risk | Rash | Screening signal in PV-005. Individual cases not yet assessed |
| Missing information | Use in pregnancy and breastfeeding | No data in this exercise |
| Missing information | Use in patients under 18 | No data in this exercise |
| Missing information | Use at 25 mg in patients aged 65 and over | Rash reports are concentrated here and no case assessment yet |

Dizziness is also listed in the reference safety information as uncommon. It is not treated as an important risk here because it was reported far less often than nausea or headache (22 reports against 89 and 88, cumulative to the data lock point in PV-007).

## 3. Pharmacovigilance activities
Plans should be proportionate to the risks of the product (R-11).

### 3.1 Routine activities
- Collect and process all reports and send them within 15 days (serious) or 30 days (non-serious) (PV-006).
- Run signal detection on every reporting period (PV-005).
- Submit periodic safety update reports on the required schedule (PV-007).

### 3.2 Additional activities proposed
| Concern | Action | Milestone |
|---|---|---|
| Rash | Review every rash case with the WHO-UMC scale and ask reporters for follow-up details such as other medicines | Before the next periodic report |
| Rash at 25 mg, age 65 and over | Propose a non-interventional study in this group | Protocol decision after case review |
| Late serious reports | Corrective action on submission process | Next reporting period |

## 4. Risk minimisation activities
Routine measures: the label and the package leaflet. Rash is not in the label, so a label update is proposed (Module 7). Any additional measure, such as a letter to healthcare professionals, is decided after case review (Module 8).

Measure of effectiveness (proposed): the number of rash reports per reporting period, compared with the 32 reported in the interval covered by PV-007.

## 5. Limits
- All data is invented, and no exposure figures exist.
- Rash is a potential risk because case assessment has not been done. It would become an identified risk only after assessment supports an association.
- The studies and milestones above are proposals, not commitments.
