This is a practice document. DMX-101 is a fictional drug and all data is invented.

## 1. Purpose
Decides whether and how to tell healthcare professionals and patients about the rash signal, and sets out the steps.

## 2. Sources and their status
- The Indian MAH guidance (R-11) says the company must have procedures for communicating safety concerns to healthcare professionals, patients and regulators, and lists a letter to healthcare professionals as a possible additional risk minimisation measure. It does not say who approves such a letter, how fast it must go out, or what it must contain.
- EU good pharmacovigilance practice Module XV (R-07) was used as a model for the letter structure and the effectiveness measures. It is a European guideline and is not an Indian requirement. Its approval steps for EU procedures are not used here.

## 3. Decision
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is a letter to healthcare professionals needed now? | Not yet. Rash is a potential risk and the cases have not been assessed (PV-008). |
| What happens if the case review supports an association and the label change is agreed (PV-009)? | Send the letter (PV-011) to prescribers, together with the label change. |
| What do patients receive? | The updated leaflet wording in PV-009. |

## 4. Audience and channel
| Audience | Channel |
|---|---|
| Doctors who prescribe diabetes medicines | Letter by post or email from the company |
| Pharmacists | Same letter |
| Patients | Updated leaflet |
| Regulator | Draft letter and plan shared before it is sent |

## 5. Steps and order
1. Finish case review of rash reports (PV-008).
2. Agree the label wording and the letter with the regulator. The route is not specified in the guidance and must be confirmed.
3. Send the letter to healthcare professionals before, or at the same time as, any public statement.
4. Update the leaflet and record the new label version (registers/label_history.csv).
5. Report on the communication in the next periodic safety report (PV-007).

## 6. Measuring whether it worked
- Number of healthcare professionals who received the letter, and any problems delivering it.
- Number of rash reports per reporting period, compared with the 32 in the interval covered by PV-007.
- Any questions or complaints received after the letter.

## 7. Limits
- All data is invented and no real healthcare professionals exist in this exercise.
- Timelines for sending the letter cannot be set because the Indian guidance gives none.
- The plan has not been reviewed by a regulatory expert.
