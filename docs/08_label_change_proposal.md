This is a practice document. DMX-101 is a fictional drug and all data is invented.

## 1. Purpose
Proposes wording to add rash to the reference safety information and the patient leaflet, and records which documents would need to change.

## 2. Why a change is proposed
- Rash is not in the current reference safety information (PV-001).
- Rash was the most frequently reported event not in the label and met the screening rule, mainly at 25 mg and in patients aged 65 and over (PV-005).
- Rash is recorded as an important potential risk in the risk management plan (PV-008).
- This proposal depends on the case review in PV-008. If the cases do not support an association, the wording below should not be used.

## 3. Proposed wording for healthcare professionals
### 3.1 Undesirable effects
| Group | Event | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Skin | Rash | Not known |

Frequency is given as not known because this exercise has no patient exposure data (PV-007, section 6), so a frequency cannot be estimated.

### 3.2 Special warnings and precautions
"Rash has been reported in patients taking DMX-101, mainly at the 25 mg dose and in patients aged 65 years and over. Patients should be told to contact their doctor if a rash appears."

## 4. Proposed wording for patients (leaflet)
"Tell your doctor if you get a rash while taking DMX-101. This has been reported most often in people aged 65 or over and in people taking the 25 mg tablet."

## 5. Documents to update if the change is approved
| Document | Action |
|---|---|
| Reference safety information | Add the wording in section 3 |
| Package insert and patient leaflet | Add the wording in sections 3 and 4 |
| Next periodic safety report | Show the change in the section on changes to reference safety information and attach the new version |
| Risk management plan summary | Update rash from potential risk to identified risk only if case review supports it |
| Label history register | Add the new version (registers/label_history.csv) |

## 6. Limits
- The Indian MAH guidance (R-11) asks the company to assess the need for changes to the reference product information after a periodic report and to implement safety changes according to urgency. It does not give a submission route, form or timeline for a label change, and it does not define a significant change. These must be confirmed with the regulator before real use.
- A 2026 draft amendment on post-approval changes to the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules was found through a secondary summary only. It was not relied on, and it was not checked for labelling changes.
- The wording is a draft and has not been reviewed by a medical or regulatory expert.
