## Purpose
Describes how the due date for each fictional DMX-101 report is worked out and checked.

## Rules used
| Report type | Limit | Counted from |
|---|---|---|
| Serious | 15 calendar days | Date the report was received |
| Non-serious | 30 calendar days | Date the report was received |

Source: Pharmacovigilance Guidance Document for Marketing Authorization Holders, Version 2.0, section 2.8 (see the references register, R-06). The cover of that document shows no effective date, so its current status should be confirmed.

## Method
1. Take the receipt date of each report.
2. Treat a report as serious if it is marked serious or its outcome is Fatal.
3. Add 15 days (serious) or 30 days (non-serious) to get the due date.
4. Compare with the date the report was sent. A report sent after the due date is late.

## Result
Of 59 serious reports, 26 were sent late (55.9% on time). All 441 non-serious reports were on time. The submission dates are invented for this exercise.

## Limits
- The guidance does not define receipt in detail. Day zero here is the recorded receipt date.
- The guidance also contains a separate 15-day rule for serious unexpected reactions in section 3.3, which this procedure does not separate out.
- Follow-up information can restart a clock. This procedure does not model follow-ups.
- Weekends and holidays are not treated differently because the guidance counts calendar days.

## Proposed next steps
- Investigate why serious reports were sent late and record a corrective action.
- Add follow-up reports and unexpected-reaction status in a later version.
